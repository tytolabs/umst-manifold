## 1. Literature: staggered vs monolithic damage–mechanics

### 1.1 Phase-field damage and variational structure

**AT1 / AT2 phase-field models** regularize brittle fracture with a smooth damage field \(d\) and a length scale \(l\); the tensile elastic energy is degraded by \(g(d)\) while a fracture term penalizes gradients of \(d\) and enforces dissipation tied to \(G_c\). **Miehe, Hofacker, Welschinger** (*Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Engrg.*, 2010, “A phase field model for rate-independent crack propagation”) give a **thermodynamically consistent** incremental formulation with **history-type irreversibility** for the tensile driving variable—standard reference for spectral elastic split + damage evolution in quasi-static brittle fracture.

**Bourdin–Francfort–Marigo (BFM)** (*J. Elasticity*, 2000; related numerical treatments) motivate the **variational fracture** viewpoint: global energy minimization / \(\Gamma\)-convergence links the regularized functional to sharp cracks as \(l \to 0\) with mesh refinement—relevant to the repo’s **multi-\((l_0,h)\)** deferral, not yet a CI harness.

### 1.2 Staggered (operator-split) vs monolithic

- **Staggered / alternate minimization:** At each load or pseudo-time increment, iterate **(i)** equilibrium at **fixed** damage \(d^k\) (elastic subproblem with degraded stiffness \(g(d^k)\)), then **(ii)** damage update at **fixed** strain \(\varepsilon^{k+1}\) from the displacement field. This is an **operator split**. It is attractive because each sub-step reuses **black-box** elasticity and a **local or graph-smoothed** damage solver (as in the shipped AT2 relaxation).

- **Monolithic:** Solve for \((u,d)\) jointly (Newton or alternate direction with consistent tangents). Offers better stability in highly coupled regimes but requires **coupled Jacobians** or sophisticated line search—**out of scope** for the current `PhaseFieldFractureSolver` (inner relaxation only).

### 1.3 Operator-split stability (sketch)

Staggered schemes can **lag** the coupling between elastic energy and damage, which may manifest as **energy drift** or **mesh-dependent** dissipation unless outer iterations or small increments are used. Practical mitigations used in the literature and in engineering codes include:

- **Multiple staggered passes per increment** (outer \(k\) loop) until a **coupled residual** or **energy stationarity** test passes.
- **Adaptive stepping** in load or pseudo-time when the damage increment is large.
- **Implicit** treatment of the **most stiff** component (often displacement) with an **explicit** damage map, or vice versa, depending on the regime—here elasticity is quasi-static linear in \(u\) at fixed \(d\), damage is nonlinear but local/smoothed on the graph.

The shipped code does **not** yet certify energy monotone bands across \((l_0,h)\); that matches **DEFERRAL — Fracture** (“energy monotone band on benchmark chain”).

## 2. Current code hooks

### 2.1 `PhaseFieldFractureSolver` (`fracture_field.rs`)

| Mechanism | Role |
|-----------|------|
| **`update_damage`** | Inner AT2-style relaxation at **fixed** \(\varepsilon\): spectral tensile \(\psi^+\) via cyclic Jacobi on \(3\times3\) blocks, graph Laplacian on \(d\), red–black under-relaxation, clamp \([0,1]\), then **`max(d_{\mathrm{old}}, d_{\mathrm{trial}})`** for irreversibility. |
| **`update_damage_staggered`** | **Outer** loop only: for each `outer_iterations`, sets `strain = strain_fn(&damage)`, then `damage ← update_damage(strain, damage, …)`. Does **not** call mechanics; the strain provider is the **orchestrator hook**. |
| **`g(d)`** | Documented as \((1-d)^2 + \eta\) for future tight coupling; **not** used inside `update_damage` (strain-only drive for \(d\)). |
| **BFM / Γ** | Verification toward Γ-convergence is **deferred** (see status doc); inner admissibility smoke exists. |

### 2.2 `VectorMechanicsSolver` (`mechanics.rs`)

| Mechanism | Role |
|-----------|------|
| **`packed_bar_network_equilibrium`** | Assembles axial \(k_e \propto (EA/L)\cdot\bigl((1-d_{\mathrm{edge}})^2 + \text{DAMAGE\_REG}\bigr)\) with \(d\) averaged to edges; **projected CG** on the Dirichlet subspace (`boundary_mask`). Optional **Jacobi diagonal preconditioner** (`use_preconditioner`). |
| **`solve_equilibrium`** | Public entry: returns \((u,\sigma)\) with **bar-network** rank-one nodal stress. |
| **`voigt_strain_from_edge_displacement`** | Nodal Voigt \(\varepsilon\) `[B,N,6]` from edge data \((\mathbf u_{\mathrm{tgt}}-\mathbf u_{\mathrm{src}})\cdot\hat{\mathbf t}\), rank-one spread, degree-normalized sum—natural bridge from \(u\) to **symmetric tensor** \(\varepsilon\) for fracture. |
| **Q1 hex / PCG** | **Separate** path: `q1_hex_elasticity::hex_solve_pcg_masked` and `ExtrudedPlateMechanics` (plate / brick scaffold). Not invoked by `PhaseFieldFractureSolver`; future staggered coupling could swap the **elasticity kernel** while keeping the same **`strain_fn`** contract. |

Degradation in mechanics matches the **square** \((1-d)^2\) spirit (plus `DAMAGE_REG`), consistent with the documented \(g(d)\) in fracture docs.

### 2.3 THMC strain wiring (`thmc.rs`)

Inside `ThmcSolver::step` (`thmc-coupled`, which **pulls** `fracture-at2`):

1. **Mechanics:** If `manifold.node_positions` is `[N,3]` and BC mask shapes align, **`VectorMechanicsSolver::solve_equilibrium`** runs with **`damage_m`** (first damage channel) and updates `state.mechanical.displacement`.
2. **Fracture:** **`strain_tensor_from_manifold`** reads **`manifold.matrix_features[.., 0, ..]`** reshaped to `[B,N,3,3]` if dimensions agree; **otherwise zeros** (documented)—so **elastic strain from the bar solve is not automatically fed** into fracture unless the cartridge fills `matrix_features` or the pipeline is refactored to call `voigt_strain_from_edge_displacement` after mechanics.

This gap is exactly the **“fresh \(\varepsilon(u)\) each mechanics solve”** item in **DEFERRAL — Fracture**: the **intended** staggered loop should refresh \(\varepsilon\) from **\(u\)** after each equilibrium at fixed \(d\), not only from a static manifold slice.

## 3. Blueprint: outer-loop API

### 3.1 Target physics (one quasi-static increment)

Given body force \(\mathbf f\), boundary data, and material parameters:

1. Initialize \(d^{(0)}\) (e.g. previous increment).
2. **For** \(k = 0,1,\ldots,K-1\) **or until convergence:**

   **Outer A — Elasticity at fixed damage:**  
   Solve for \(\mathbf u^{(k+1)}\) such that equilibrium holds with stiffness degraded by \(g(d^{(k)})\), using the shipped **bar network** (today) or a **hex continuum** kernel later. Recover **nodal** \(\varepsilon^{(k+1)} = \varepsilon(\mathbf u^{(k+1)})\) (e.g. Voigt map → symmetric \(3\times3\)).

   **Outer B — Damage at fixed strain:**  
   \(d^{(k+1)} = \texttt{update\_damage}\bigl(\varepsilon^{(k+1)},\, d^{(k)},\,G_c,\,l,\,\texttt{edges\_b1}\bigr)\).

3. **Irreversibility** is already enforced **inside** `update_damage` via `max(d_old, d_trial)` after inner relaxation.

### 3.2 Call-site shape (already partially available)

- **`update_damage_staggered(strain_fn, damage0, gc, edges, outer_iterations)`** where `strain_fn(&d)` **must** implement: “solve mechanics with **this** \(d\)” → build \(\varepsilon(\mathbf u)\) → return `[B,N,3,3]` for fracture.

Today **`strain_fn` is generic**; the missing piece is a **single authoritative helper** (e.g. on `ThmcSolver` or a small orchestrator) that runs `VectorMechanicsSolver::solve_equilibrium` with `damage = d`, forms \(\varepsilon\) from **`voigt_strain_from_edge_displacement`** + tensor packing, and optionally writes displacement into `ThmcState` / manifold for downstream physics.

### 3.3 Termination criteria (recommended)

| Criterion | Formula / action |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Displacement–damage residual** | \(\|\mathbf u^{(k+1)}-\mathbf u^{(k)}\|_\infty\) and \(\|d^{(k+1)}-d^{(k)}\|_\infty\) below `tol_u`, `tol_d`. |
| **Energy band** | Compare discrete mechanical work + regularized fracture functional increment to a **monotone band** (deferral acceptance). |
| **Cap** | `max_outer` to avoid infinite splits when softening is strong. |

Host-side scalar reads for early exit are **acceptable in orchestration / tests** even when inner kernels stay device-tensor-pure (THMC already documents tradeoffs for Newton early exit).
